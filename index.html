Understanding the National Electrical Code (NEC) math written into Virginia HB 395, Maryland HB 1532, Colorado HB 26-1007, and New Jersey S 2368. A standard US household branch circuit is rated at 15 Amps × 120 Volts = 1,800W. Under NEC continuous-load and 120% busbar/branch backfeed allowances, injecting up to 3.26 Amps × 120V = 391.2W cannot overload a 15A household circuit—even if other appliances or electronics are running on that same breaker. Several state legislatures explicitly exempted systems ≤ 391W from building-wiring modifications and certain UL 3700 listing rules so apartment renters can safely plug a single 400W panel + 360W–391W microinverter into any standard outlet.
